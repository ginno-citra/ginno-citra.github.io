Customer aging dashboard

A Power BI report that shows which customers owe money and how long it has been outstanding. A summary page gives managers the big picture, and a detail page drills down to individual ledger entries.

Tools: Power BI, DAX, Power Query Report date: April 30, 2025 Pages: Aging Summary, Aging Detail

The problem

Statements of account are sent in advance each month, and the ledger covers several years of charges and payments. Managers need to see who is overdue, and by how much, without searching through thousands of rows. Billers, the accounting supervisor and managers, and operations use it for their monthly report.

The data

The report is built on customer ledger entries linked to a customer list, a units list, tenant charge types, and a date table. All names and amounts shown are anonymized.

Aging Summary: KPI cards for net balance, past due, advance payments and % over 90 days, with past due broken down by customer type, charge type, aging bucket and building, plus the top 10 owners.

Data model: a star schema

The report uses a star schema. Customer Ledger Entries is the fact table, and four dimension tables describe it: CustomerList, TenantCharge, UnitList and DateTable. Every relationship is one-to-many and filters in one direction, from the dimension to the ledger, so slicers and visuals behave predictably.

The aging bucket and its sort order are calculated columns in the ledger table.

Model view: Customer Ledger Entries (the fact table) connected to four dimension tables. Click the image to see it full size.

Design decisions

Each decision below answers a real question about how the numbers should be read.

Aging by billing month

Buckets follow the billing month rather than exact day counts. April 2025 billing falls in 0–30, March in 31–60, and so on, which matches how statements are sent. I built the buckets with SWITCH in a DAX calculated column, plus a second column to control the sort order.

Charges due on the report date

Anything due on the report date, April 30, 2025, goes in the 1–30 days bucket instead of Current, so charges due that day are treated as owed, not hidden.

Posting Date instead of Due Date

Some Due Date values are blank in the ledger, so I used Posting Date for the aging and detail tables. This keeps every row in a bucket.

Advance payments kept separate

Payments made ahead of time are labeled as advance payments and shown as their own KPI, so they don't reduce the past-due totals. Net Balance then combines the two: past due plus advance payments.

Top 10 shows owners only

The developer owns many unsold units and carries one very large balance, so the Top 10 table excludes it and says so in a note below the table. The developer's balance still appears in the Past Due by Customer Type chart.

A target for % over 90 days

The % over 90 days card divides Over 90 Days by Total Past Due and compares it with a 50% target. At 84.7% it is currently flagged Critical, so the problem stands out right away.

Drillthrough to invoice detail

On the summary page, right-click a customer to open the Aging Detail page filtered to that customer, showing the individual ledger entries behind the numbers.

Aging Detail: ledger entries for the selected customer, grouped by aging bucket.
